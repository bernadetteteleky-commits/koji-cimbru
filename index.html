About the project

An old technique, a new raw material

Koji is a culture of Aspergillus oryzae, traditionally used in fermented foods based on cereals and soy. The proteases and lipases it produces release free amino acids, peptides and aroma compounds. The meat becomes more tender and its umami taste intensifies.

The process is poorly documented for pork and beef, particularly regarding the compounds formed and food safety. The project develops and validates two technological applications that the restaurant Cimbru will integrate into its own kitchen.

The research is carried out by a team from the Faculty of Food Science and Technology at USAMV Cluj-Napoca, from laboratory trials to validation under real conditions of use.

Objectives

What we set out to do

A controlled koji fermentation technology for pork and beef dishes that improves sensory quality, shortens ageing, and increases microbiological safety and stability.

O1

Process development and optimisation

Setting the technological parameters (culture type and dose, time, temperature, humidity) for two applications: pork, targeting faster tenderisation and umami taste, and beef, targeting aromatic complexity, texture and stability.

O2

Biochemical and microbiological characterisation

Physico-chemical parameters (pH, water activity, proximate analysis, colour), microbiological analysis for food safety, the effect of cooking, and the profile of the compounds formed (free amino acids, peptides, metabolites), including by HPLC.

O3

Sensory evaluation and validation

Texture, aroma, taste and overall acceptability assessed with a 9-point hedonic test, correlated with the analytical data and compared with traditional processing methods.

O4

Technology transfer and exploitation

Technological protocols, technical sheets and implementation guides, plus validation of the applications under real conditions of use at Cimbru.

Activities

From the laboratory to the kitchen in 6 months

  1. Fermentation protocols

    Developing and optimising the protocols for pork and beef.

  2. Experimental batches

    Producing batches of fermented products alongside control samples.

  3. Characterisation and evaluation

    Physico-chemical, microbiological and biochemical analyses, followed by sensory evaluation.

  4. Validation and transfer

    Validation in the Cimbru kitchen, product technical sheets, and transfer to the beneficiary.

Results

What the project leaves behind

  • at least two experimentally validated fermentation protocols;
  • product technical sheets and implementation guides for the beneficiary;
  • research reports on the composition, safety and sensory quality of the products;
  • a technology at TRL 5, integrated into the restaurant's operations.

Beneficiary

Cimbru · CIMBRU GOURMET SRL

Cimbru began as a food truck in Cluj-Napoca and grew into a restaurant and delivery service. Since autumn 2021, chef Nicolae Crăciun has also run Cimbru Author Cuisine on Strada Emil Isac 11, a casual fine dining restaurant whose short menu changes every week with the season and works closely with local producers.

In 2026 it became the first restaurant in Romania listed in the international We're Smart Green Guide, with a rating of one radish, and joined the JRE (Jeunes Restaurateurs) association.
